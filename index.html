The shift, in six moves

What changed, and what it means for a studio.

01

Audiences moved to streaming, YouTube and creators.

Streaming now takes nearly half of all US TV time. YouTube alone is the biggest distributor on the TV set. YouTube says the TV is now the most-watched screen for more than half of its top 100 creators.

The living room is open to anyone who can hold an audience. That includes your competitors, and your next partners.

02

Brand money followed them.

US advertisers will spend about $44 billion with creators in 2026, more than three times 2021. Brands are also financing film and TV directly: Sugar23 and Fifth Season set out to co-finance more than $100 million of production with brand partners, who take ownership instead of paying a fee.

Brands are buying entertainment people choose to watch. Studios that can make it, and sell it, get paid twice: for the work and for the IP.

03

Unscripted got more important, and harder.

Unscripted is now the majority of what Netflix orders. In 2025 a dating show, Love Island USA, became the first unscripted series to top the year’s streaming originals. Yet unscripted production volume on streaming platforms fell 31% the same year.

Buyers want more unscripted, from fewer shows. Focus wins.

The unscripted practice

04

The middle got squeezed.

US film and sound recording employment is down more than a quarter from its 2022 peak. Reality shoot days in Los Angeles fell 40% in the second quarter of 2026, and commercial shoot days fell by more than a fifth. Agency holding companies are taking more production in-house.

Waiting for volume to come back is not a plan. Studios need new ways to earn.

05

Ownership is where the value is.

The Super Mario Galaxy Movie, built on Nintendo’s IP, was the first release of 2026 to pass $1 billion worldwide. In 2024, according to investor documents reported by Bloomberg, MrBeast’s snack brand made a profit while his media business, on similar sales, lost almost $80 million.

The asset is the IP and the audience, not the episode. Studios paid only a fee keep the thinnest slice.

06

AI changed the cost and speed of making.

Netflix says generative AI workflows have been used in roughly 300 of its titles in 2026, mostly in post-production, and that one sequence in El Eternauta was finished ten times faster. The makers of Amazon’s House of David say they used it in an estimated 350 to 400 shots in season two.

The new guild contracts set the rules: consent first. The studios that rebuild around their own data, responsibly, will make more for less.
